The construction

A short proof with an exact boundary

Rational points are placed on four fixed lines. Every admissible pair selects one point from each family, and one common dilation converts the whole configuration to integer coordinates.

01

Choose the four families

Place indexed points on Y=0, X=0, Y=X, and Y=−X. Their family sizes add to exactly n.

02

Force four points onto one line

The line through the two axis points is (3n+x)X + (n+y)Y = 1. The diagonal points lie on it precisely when z=x+y and w=x−y.

03

Count — and keep the correction

A 28-class integer count yields the closed formula. The essential parameter is v = ⌈(s−d−1)/2⌉: replacing d by c already loses a line at n=29.

Two immediate improvements

The construction sharpens the recorded best-known values.

These are rigorous lower bounds, not optimality claims. Larger configurations may still exist.
