# Exact metadata for proof and prior-work references

The proof depends on exactly two external mathematical results.

1. Henri Darmon and Loïc Merel, “Winding quotients and some variants of
   Fermat's Last Theorem,” *Journal für die reine und angewandte Mathematik*
   **490** (1997), 81–100. DOI:
   <https://doi.org/10.1515/crll.1997.490.81>. The used statement is part 1 of
   the Main Theorem: for exponent `n>=3`, `x^n+y^n=2z^n` has no nontrivial
   primitive integer solution. With `n=3`, this excludes a nonconstant
   three-term rational AP of cubes after the common-cube denominator clearing
   stated in the manuscript.

2. Lajos Hajdu and Szabolcs Tengely, “Powers in arithmetic progressions,”
   *The Ramanujan Journal* **55** (2021), 965–986. DOI:
   <https://doi.org/10.1007/s11139-020-00331-5>. The used result is Theorem 2
   specialized to `N=5`, which gives `P_5(3)=3` for ordinary cubes. The
   publisher records publication on 30 January 2021 and issue date August 2021.

The related-work section additionally cites the following original papers.
They are not imported as proof lemmas.

3. Enrique Gonzalez-Jimenez and Xavier Xarles, “Five squares in arithmetic
   progression over quadratic fields,” *Revista Matematica Iberoamericana*
   **29** (2013), 1211--1238. DOI:
   <https://doi.org/10.4171/RMI/754>. This is the closest methodological
   predecessor: it uses quadratic Kummer types, common scaling, reversal,
   rational curves, and local solubility, while requiring all five entries to
   be squares in a quadratic field.

4. Enrique Gonzalez-Jimenez, “Three cubes in arithmetic progression over
   quadratic fields,” *Archiv der Mathematik* **95** (2010), 233--241. DOI:
   <https://doi.org/10.1007/s00013-010-0166-5>.

5. Lajos Hajdu and Szabolcs Tengely, “Arithmetic progressions of squares,
   cubes and n-th powers,” *Functiones et Approximatio Commentarii
   Mathematici* **41** (2009), 129--138. DOI:
   <https://doi.org/10.7169/facm/1261157805>.

6. Nils Bruin, Kalman Gyory, Lajos Hajdu, and Szabolcs Tengely, “Arithmetic
   progressions consisting of unlike powers,” *Indagationes Mathematicae*
   **17** (2006), 539--555. DOI:
   <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0019-3577(07)00002-X>.

7. Xavier Xarles, “Squares in arithmetic progression over number fields,”
   *Journal of Number Theory* **132** (2012), 379--389. DOI:
   <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jnt.2011.07.010>.

8. Andrew Bremner and Samir Siksek, “Squares in arithmetic progression over
   cubic fields,” *International Journal of Number Theory* **12** (2016),
   1409--1414. DOI:
   <https://doi.org/10.1142/S179304211650086X>.
