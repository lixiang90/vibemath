\begin{abstract}
For a nonconstant rational five-term arithmetic progression, we ask how many
nonzero terms can, after multiplication by one common rational scalar, be
cubes in a single nontrivial pure cubic field.  We prove that the exact
maximum is four.  The proof begins by determining the kernel of the map from
rational cube classes to the Kummer group of $\Q(\sqrt[3]{D})$.  After
normalizing radicands and quotienting by affine color change and reversal, we
obtain exactly 25 five-position color orbits.  Ten orbits are excluded by
known theorems on rational cubes in arithmetic progression.  A prime-support
lemma reduces the remaining 15 orbits to 60 explicit systems of three
diagonal cubic equations.  For every system, exhaustive finite-field
enumeration gives an obstruction at a displayed prime of good reduction; a
versioned certificate records all $p^2-1$ nonzero parameter pairs.  The
progression $(-3,-1,1,3,5)$ supplies four counted terms over
$\Q(\sqrt[3]{3})$.  Thus $R^{\times}_{(3,1)}(5)=4$.  This finite
classification excludes every five-hit color pattern but does not classify
all rational progressions attaining four hits.  We additionally
prove that two positive-rank genus-one curves supply infinite families in
six distinct four-hit orbits.  Four arise from
$2X^3-3Y^3+Z^3=0$; the two further orbits
$((0,1,2,4),0010)$ and $((0,1,3,4),0010)$ arise from
$3X^3-4Y^3+Z^3=0$.  These six branches are infinite.  Separately, the
genus-four model $((0,1,2,4),0102)$ contains the explicit nondegenerate
maximizer $(64,36,8,-20,-48)$ over $\Q(\sqrt[3]{6})$; its elliptic
quotient has a certified non-torsion point, but no infinite family on the
source curve is claimed.  Thus seven of the initial 31 four-hit models are
closed for existence and 24 remain open.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} arithmetic progression; pure cubic field;
Kummer class; perfect cube; local obstruction.

\section{Definition and normalizations}
Let $A_i=a+id$ for $0\leq i<5$, where $a,d\in\Q$ and $d\ne0$.  For a
nontrivial class $[D]\in\Q^*/\Q^{*3}$ put
$K_D=\Q(\sqrt[3]{D})$.  Define $R^{\times}_{(3,1)}(5)$ as the maximum number
of nonzero $A_i$ for which there are one $D$ and one $\lambda\in\Q^*$ such
that $\lambda A_i\in K_D^3$ at every counted position.

The radicand may be chosen as a positive cube-free integer: $-1$ is already
a rational cube, and multiplying $D$ by a rational cube changes no field.
Moreover $D$ and $D^2$ generate the same field.  We quotient progressions by
common rational scaling and by reversal
$(a,d)\mapsto(a+4d,-d)$.  Zero has no class in $\Q^*/\Q^{*3}$ and is never
counted.  If $D$ is a rational cube, then $K_D=\Q$ and the problem is the
ordinary rational-cube problem, whose five-term maximum is three
\cite{HT}; this degree-one degeneration cannot attain our maximum.

\section{The pure-cubic Kummer kernel}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:kernel}
If $[D]\ne1$ in $\Q^*/\Q^{*3}$, then
\[
\ker\bigl(\Q^*/\Q^{*3}\longrightarrow K_D^*/K_D^{*3}\bigr)
=\langle[D]\rangle=\{1,[D],[D]^2\}.
\]
Equivalently, a nonzero rational $r$ is a cube in $K_D$ if and only if
$[r]\in\langle[D]\rangle$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $\alpha^3=D$ and $y=a+b\alpha+c\alpha^2$.  Reduction of $y^3$ in the
basis $1,\alpha,\alpha^2$ gives nonconstant coefficients
\begin{align*}
3(a^2b+Da c^2+Db^2c),\qquad
3(a^2c+ab^2+Dbc^2).
\end{align*}
If $a=0$, their vanishing implies $bc=0$, so $y$ lies on either the
$\Q\alpha$ or $\Q\alpha^2$ axis.  If $a\ne0$, put $B=b/a$, $C=c/a$.  The two
equations are
\[
B+DC^2+DB^2C=0,\qquad C+B^2+DBC^2=0.
\]
Their resultants, eliminating $C$ and $B$ respectively, are
\[
BD(B^3D-1)^2,\qquad C(C^3D^2-1)^2.
\]
Since $D$ is not a rational cube, the first forces $B=0$, and then the first
equation forces $C=0$.  Hence an element of $K_D$ with rational cube lies in
exactly one of $\Q$, $\Q\alpha$, $\Q\alpha^2$.  Cubing proves the asserted
three classes, and the converse is immediate.
\end{proof}

Thus, after the common scaling, every counted term has a unique color
$c_i\in\F_3$ and can be written
\begin{equation}\label{eq:colors}
A_i=D^{c_i}x_i^3,\qquad x_i\in\Q^*.
\end{equation}
Changing the common scale by $D^k$, replacing $D$ by $D^2$, and reversing
the progression act on color words by
\[
c_i\mapsto c_i+k,\qquad c_i\mapsto-c_i,\qquad
(c_0,\ldots,c_4)\mapsto(c_4,\ldots,c_0).
\]

\section{The 25 color orbits}
Exact enumeration gives 25 orbits of $\F_3^5$ under these operations.  For
completeness, Burnside's lemma gives the same count without trusting the
choice of representatives: the fixed-point counts of the 12 group elements
are
\[
243,27,9,9,9,1,1,1,0,0,0,0.
\]
Their sum is 300, and hence there are $300/12=25$ orbits.  Nine
contain a monochromatic three-term arithmetic progression of indices among
\[
(0,1,2),(1,2,3),(2,3,4),(0,2,4).
\]
Such an orbit would give three nonconstant rational cubes in arithmetic
progression after scaling, contrary to Darmon--Merel \cite{DM}.  Exactly one
further orbit, represented by $00100$, survives this test but has four terms
of one color, at positions $0,1,3,4$.  It contradicts the exact ordinary
bound $P_5(3)=3$ of Hajdu--Tengely \cite{HT}.  The remaining representatives
are listed below.  There is no hidden integrality assumption here: choosing
an integer $L$ that clears the finitely many rational cube roots and the
progression parameters, multiplication of the entire progression by the
common cube $L^3$ makes both the progression and those roots integral.
Cube positions and nonconstancy are therefore preserved.
\begin{gather*}
00101,00102,00110,00112,00120,00121,00122,01001,01002,01012,\\
01021,01102,01120,01201,01210.                         \tag{1}
\end{gather*}

\section{Prime support and the 60 curves}
Clear denominators and divide by the common gcd, obtaining a primitive
integer progression $A_i=a+id$.  Its classes have the affine form
$g+c_i\delta$ in $\Q^*/\Q^{*3}$.  For $i\ne j$,
\[
\gcd(A_i,A_j)\mid |i-j|,
\]
because $\gcd(a,d)=1$.  Therefore a prime $p>3$ divides at most one of the
five terms.

\begin{lemma}[prime-support lemma]\label{lem:support}
For each of the 15 words in (1), every $p>3$ occurs in every $A_i$ with
exponent divisible by three.  Consequently both $g$ and $\delta$ are
supported only at $2$ and $3$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If a $p>3$ exponent were nonzero modulo three, the five valuation residues
would be supported at one position.  But they must be the values of the
affine function $c\mapsto g_p+c\delta_p$ on the displayed color word.
For a two-color word in (1), both color blocks have size at least two, so a
singleton support is impossible.  For a three-color word, an affine function
over $\F_3$ vanishing at the other two colors vanishes identically.  This is
again impossible.  Hence all five residues vanish.  Taking differences of
two used colors proves the assertion for $\delta$, and then for $g$.
\end{proof}

After another common rational scaling removes $g$, the four possible
one-dimensional subspaces of the $\{2,3\}$-supported class plane have
representatives
\[
D=2,3,6,18.
\]
For a word $c=(c_0,\ldots,c_4)$ and one such $D$, all five hits give the
concrete projective curve in $\mathbf P^4$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:curve}
D^{c_i}x_i^3-2D^{c_{i+1}}x_{i+1}^3
 +D^{c_{i+2}}x_{i+2}^3=0\quad(0\leq i\leq2).
\end{equation}

At every prime used below, $p\geq7$ and $p\nmid D$.  These conditions really
give good reduction.  Put $u_i=D^{c_i}x_i^2$.  After deleting the invertible
factor 3, the Jacobian matrix of the three equations is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
u_0&-2u_1&u_2&0&0\\
0&u_1&-2u_2&u_3&0\\
0&0&u_2&-2u_3&u_4
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
A projective solution has at most one $u_i=0$: two zero terms in the induced
five-term progression would force both its initial term and common difference
to vanish because $p>4$.  If no $u_i$ is zero, columns $0,1,2$ give a nonzero
minor.  If the unique zero is respectively $u_0,u_1,u_2,u_3,u_4$, columns
$124,024,014,014,013$ give
\[
3u_1u_2u_4,-2u_0u_2u_4,u_0u_1u_4,u_0u_1u_4,-2u_0u_1u_3.
\]
Thus the Jacobian has rank 3 at every geometric point, proving smoothness.

\section{Exact maximum and scope of classification}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
With nonzero terms and nontrivial pure cubic fields as in Section 1,
\[
R^{\times}_{(3,1)}(5)=4.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A five-hit progression has one of the 25 color orbits.  The first ten are
excluded in Section 3.  Lemma \ref{lem:support} and the 60 good-prime
obstructions exclude the remaining 15.  Hence the maximum is at most four.
For the reverse inequality, in $K=\Q(\alpha)$ with $\alpha^3=3$, the nonzero
progression
\[
-3,-1,1,3,5
\]
has its first four terms equal to the cubes of
$-\alpha,-1,1,\alpha$.
\end{proof}

The theorem includes a complete classification and exclusion of every
\emph{five-hit color/position class}.  The next two sections close seven of
the 31 four-hit models for existence: six have proved infinite families, and
the seventh has one rigorously verified nondegenerate point.  They do not
classify every rational progression attaining four hits.  The other 24
models remain distinct Diophantine problems, and we do not replace their
rational-point classification by a bounded search.

If zero were counted, $-2,-1,0,1,2$ would give five hits in
$\Q(\sqrt[3]{2})$; this is exactly why the superscript $\times$ is necessary.

\section{Six four-hit branches on two positive-rank cubics}

\subsection{The diagonal-cubic cluster at \texorpdfstring{$u^3-243$}{u3-243}}

We strengthen Theorem~\ref{thm:main} in four of the 31
four-hit color/position models left open above.  Begin with positions
$0,1,3$ rational cubes.  Writing them as $X^3,Y^3,Z^3$, respectively, the
AP identity $A_3=3A_1-2A_0$ gives the smooth plane cubic
\begin{equation}\label{eq:C0001}
C:\qquad 2X^3-3Y^3+Z^3=0.
\end{equation}
The associated five-term progression is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:AP0001}
 (X^3,Y^3,2Y^3-X^3,Z^3,4Y^3-3X^3).
\end{equation}
The three partial derivatives of \eqref{eq:C0001} have no common projective
zero, so $C$ is a genus-one curve.  Put $O=(1:1:1)$ and use it as origin.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:0001}
The curve \eqref{eq:C0001} has positive Mordell--Weil rank.  Consequently,
in each of the four distinct color/position orbits
\[
\begin{gathered}
((0,1,3,4),0001),\qquad ((0,1,2,3),0010),\\
((0,1,2,3),0100),\qquad ((0,1,2,4),0111),
\end{gathered}
\]
there are infinitely many inequivalent nonconstant rational five-term
arithmetic progressions, with no zero term, having exactly four counted
positions in a nontrivial pure cubic field.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $(X:Y:Z)\in C$, set
\[
 \begin{split}
 N&=6X^3Y^3+3Y^3Z^3-2Z^3X^3,\\
 T&=(2X^3+3Y^3)(-3Y^3-Z^3)(Z^3-2X^3).
 \end{split}
\]
On the open set $XYZ\ne0$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mordellmap}
 \phi(X:Y:Z)=
 \left(\frac{N}{X^2Y^2Z^2},
       -\frac{T}{2X^3Y^3Z^3}\right)=(u,v).
\end{equation}
A direct expansion, followed by use of \eqref{eq:C0001}, gives
\[
 v^2=u^3-243.
\]
Equivalently, after clearing denominators the numerator of
$v^2-u^3+243$ is divisible by $2X^3-3Y^3+Z^3$; the exact quotient is checked
by the accompanying symbolic test.  Thus \eqref{eq:mordellmap} is a rational
map to
\[
 E:\quad v^2=u^3-243.
\]
A rational map from a smooth projective curve to the proper curve $E$ extends
over its apparent exceptional points.  Notice also that $C(\Q)$ has no point
with $XYZ=0$: such a point would make one of $3,-2,3/2$ a rational cube.

Let
\[
 P=(4:1:-5)\in C(\Q),\qquad Q=(7,10)\in E(\Q).
\]
Exact substitution and the tangent formula on $E$ give
\[
 \phi(O)=(7,-10)=-Q,
 \qquad
 \phi(P)=\left(\frac{16009}{400},-
 \frac{2021723}{8000}\right)=2Q.
\]
The integral equation for $E$ has discriminant
$-16\cdot27\cdot243^2=-2^4 3^{13}$.  Since $Q$ has nonzero second
coordinate and $10^2$ does not divide this discriminant, the Nagell--Lutz
theorem shows that $Q$ has infinite order.

Translate $\phi$ by $-\phi(O)$.  The resulting nonconstant morphism sends
origins to origins and hence is an isogeny of genus-one curves.  Its value at
$P$ is $2Q-(-Q)=3Q$, which is non-torsion.  Therefore $P$ has infinite order
in $C(\Q)$, and the points
\[
 P_n=O+n(P-O)\qquad(n\in\mathbf Z)
\]
are pairwise distinct.

For $n\ne0$, substitute $P_n=(X_n:Y_n:Z_n)$ in
\eqref{eq:AP0001}.  None of $X_n,Y_n,Z_n$ can vanish, as observed above;
$2Y_n^3-X_n^3=0$ would make $2$ a rational cube, while
$4Y_n^3-3X_n^3=0$ would make $4/3$ one.  Moreover the common difference
vanishes only when $X_n=Y_n=Z_n$, that is, at $P_n=O$.

The last term $A_4=4Y_n^3-3X_n^3$ is not a rational cube.  Otherwise
positions $0,1,3,4$ would be four rational cubes in a nonconstant five-term
AP, contradicting Hajdu--Tengely's bound $P_5(3)=3$.  Choose a positive
cube-free representative $D_{4,n}$ of $[A_4]$.  Then
$A_4=D_{4,n}W_{4,n}^3$ for some $W_{4,n}\in\Q^*$, and $K_{D_{4,n}}$ is
genuinely cubic.  Thus positions $0,1,3,4$ have colors $0001$.  Theorem
\ref{thm:main} prevents position 2 from being a fifth hit.

The middle term $A_2=2Y_n^3-X_n^3$ is likewise not a rational cube, since
otherwise positions $0,1,2,3$ would be four rational cubes, again
contradicting $P_5(3)=3$.  If $D_{2,n}$ is the positive cube-free
representative of $[A_2]$, then
$A_2=D_{2,n}W_{2,n}^3$ with $W_{2,n}\in\Q^*$ and $K_{D_{2,n}}$ genuinely
cubic.  Positions $0,1,2,3$ therefore have colors $0010$, and Theorem
\ref{thm:main} prevents position 4 from being a fifth hit.  These two models
are not identified by affine color change and reversal: the first omits the
central position 2, whereas the second omits an endpoint (position 4, or
position 0 after reversal).

Two further AP constructions use the same point $(X_n:Y_n:Z_n)$ of $C$:
\begin{align}
 &(Z_n^3,(Z_n^3+Y_n^3)/2,Y_n^3,X_n^3,2Y_n^3-Z_n^3),
 \label{eq:AP0100}\\
 &(2X_n^3-Y_n^3,X_n^3,Y_n^3,2Y_n^3-X_n^3,Z_n^3).
 \label{eq:AP0111}
\end{align}
The first is an AP because $X^3-3Y^3+2Z^3=0$ after swapping $X$ and
$Z$ in \eqref{eq:C0001}; its rational-cube positions are $0,2,3$.
Its singleton $A_1$ cannot vanish: otherwise $Z=-Y$ and the cubic would
give $X^3=2Y^3$.  Nor can its missing term $A_4$ vanish, since that would
give $Z^3=2Y^3$.  If $A_1$ were a rational cube, positions $0,1,2,3$
would contradict $P_5(3)=3$.  Its nontrivial cube class therefore produces
the orbit $((0,1,2,3),0100)$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:main} excludes a fifth
hit.

In the second AP the rational-cube positions are $1,2,4$.  Its singleton
$A_0$ and missing term $A_3$ are nonzero, since their vanishing would make
$2$ a rational cube.  The singleton is not a rational cube by the same
$P_5(3)=3$ argument.  Before color normalization its word is $1000$; the
affine color map $c\mapsto2c+1$ gives the representative $0111$.
Again Theorem~\ref{thm:main} excludes a fifth hit.  The exact orbit
enumeration verifies that all four displayed models are pairwise distinct.

Finally, distinct projective points of $C(\Q)$ give distinct progressions up
to common rational scaling within each construction.  Indeed, comparison of
any fixed nonzero rational-cube position makes the scale a rational cube,
after which injectivity of cubing on $\Q$ recovers the same projective
coordinates.  Reversal has fibers of size at most two, so infinitely many
equivalence classes remain after reversal.
\end{proof}

For the first nonconstant point $P$, formula \eqref{eq:AP0001} gives
\[
 (64,1,-62,-125,-188),
\]
with common difference $-63$.  If $\alpha^3=188$ and $\beta^3=62$, then the
$0001$ roots at positions $0,1,3,4$ are $4,1,-5,-\alpha$, while the $0010$
roots at positions $0,1,2,3$ are $4,1,-\beta,-5$.  These are two distinct
color/position models carried by the same rational AP.  They differ from the lower-bound progression
in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}: that progression has four consecutive counted
positions in the orbit $0110$ over $\Q(\sqrt[3]{3})$, while the present one
realizes both displayed orbits.

The same point $P$ in \eqref{eq:AP0100} and \eqref{eq:AP0111} gives the
reversed pair
\[
 (-125,-62,1,64,127),\qquad (127,64,1,-62,-125).
\]
The first realizes $0100$ over $\Q(\sqrt[3]{62})$, while the second realizes
$0111$ over $\Q(\sqrt[3]{127})$.

The construction is an integer-parameter Mordell--Weil family, made explicit
by the exact chord law in the supplement.  It is not a rational
$\mathbf P^1$ parametrization, which a smooth genus-one curve cannot admit.
We prove positive rank but do not claim the exact rank, torsion subgroup, or a
complete set of generators for $C(\Q)$.

\subsection{The second diagonal-cubic cluster}

The remaining two $3+1$ models have repeated-color positions $0,1,4$.
Writing these entries as $X^3,Y^3,Z^3$, respectively, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Csecond}
 C':\qquad 3X^3-4Y^3+Z^3=0
\end{equation}
and the progression
\begin{equation}\label{eq:APsecond}
 (X^3,Y^3,2Y^3-X^3,3Y^3-2X^3,Z^3).
\end{equation}
The partial derivatives $9X^2,-12Y^2,3Z^2$ show that $C'$ is smooth.
Use $O'=(1:1:1)$ as origin.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:second3plus1}
The curve \eqref{eq:Csecond} has positive Mordell--Weil rank.  Consequently
each of
\[
 ((0,1,2,4),0010),\qquad ((0,1,3,4),0010)
\]
contains infinitely many inequivalent nonconstant rational five-term
arithmetic progressions, with no zero term, having exactly four counted
positions in a nontrivial pure cubic field.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $(X:Y:Z)\in C'$, put
\[
\begin{aligned}
 N'&=12X^3Y^3+4Y^3Z^3-3Z^3X^3,\\
 T'&=(3X^3+4Y^3)(-4Y^3-Z^3)(Z^3-3X^3).
\end{aligned}
\]
On $XYZ\ne0$ define
\[
 \phi'(X:Y:Z)=
 \left(\frac{N'}{X^2Y^2Z^2},
       -\frac{T'}{2X^3Y^3Z^3}\right)=(u,v).
\]
The exact symbolic certificate verifies
\[
 (T')^2-4(N')^3+3888X^6Y^6Z^6
 =(3X^3-4Y^3+Z^3)H'(X,Y,Z).
\]
Thus $\phi'$ maps to
\[
 E':\quad v^2=u^3-972.
\]
There is no rational point of $C'$ with $XYZ=0$, since such a point would
make one of $4,-3,4/3$ a rational cube.  The displayed rational map therefore
has no rational exceptional point; more generally it extends from the smooth
projective curve $C'$ to the proper curve $E'$.

Let
\[
 P'=(5:2:-7)\in C'(\Q),\qquad Q'=(13,35)\in E'(\Q).
\]
Exact substitution and the tangent law on $E'$ give
\[
 \phi'(O')=(13,-35)=-Q',\qquad
 \phi'(P')=
 \left(\frac{129649}{4900},-\frac{45441143}{343000}\right)=2Q'.
\]
The integral equation for $E'$ has discriminant
\[
 -16\cdot27\cdot972^2=-2^8 3^{13}.
\]
Because $35^2\nmid -2^8 3^{13}$, Nagell--Lutz shows that $Q'$ has infinite
order.  Translating $\phi'$ by $-\phi'(O')$ gives a nonconstant morphism
sending origins to origins, hence an isogeny.  It sends $P'-O'$ to
$2Q'-(-Q')=3Q'$, so $P'-O'$ is non-torsion.

Put $P'_n=O'+n(P'-O')=(X_n:Y_n:Z_n)$.  These points are pairwise distinct.
For $n\ne0$, \eqref{eq:APsecond} is nonconstant.  Its three rational-cube
entries are nonzero because $C'(\Q)$ has no point with $XYZ=0$; its two
middle entries are nonzero because their vanishing would make $2$ or $3/2$
a rational cube.  Neither middle entry is a rational cube, since that would
produce four rational cubes in a five-term rational AP, contrary to
$P_5(3)=3$.

Choose positive cube-free representatives $D_{2,n}$ and $D_{3,n}$ of the
classes of the entries in positions 2 and 3.  The Kummer-kernel lemma shows
that over $\Q(\sqrt[3]{D_{2,n}})$ the positions $0,1,2,4$ have colors
$0010$, while over $\Q(\sqrt[3]{D_{3,n}})$ the positions $0,1,3,4$ have
colors $0010$.  Both fields are nontrivial.  Theorem~\ref{thm:main} excludes
a fifth hit in either field.

Finally, proportional progressions from \eqref{eq:APsecond} make the common
scale a rational cube by comparison at position 0; comparison at positions
1 and 4 then recovers the same projective point of $C'$.  Reversal has fibers
of size at most two, so infinitely many equivalence classes remain.
\end{proof}

The point $P'$ gives
\[
 (125,8,-109,-226,-343).
\]
Over $\Q(\sqrt[3]{109})$ this realizes $((0,1,2,4),0010)$, and over
$\Q(\sqrt[3]{226})$ it realizes $((0,1,3,4),0010)$.  The exact supplement
checks the two cube-class vectors
\[
 (0,0,1,\ast,0),\qquad (0,0,\ast,1,0),
\]
where $\ast$ denotes a non-hit.


\section{A seventh four-hit model: strict existence}

\begin{theorem}[Existence in the $0102$ model]\label{thm:0102existence}
The genus-four model $((0,1,2,4),0102)$ contains a nonconstant rational
five-term arithmetic progression with no zero term and exactly four cube
positions in the genuine cubic field $\Q(\sqrt[3]{6})$.  Its natural
elliptic quotient has positive Mordell--Weil rank.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $A_0=X^3$ and $A_2=Y^3$.  Then
\[
 A_1=(X^3+Y^3)/2,\qquad A_4=2Y^3-X^3.
\]
The singleton positions 1 and 4 have inverse Kummer classes precisely on
the weighted-projective curve
\begin{equation}\label{eq:C0102existence}
 (X^3+Y^3)(2Y^3-X^3)=2W^3.
\end{equation}
This is the smooth genus-four cyclic cover reconstructed in the supplement.
The point $(X:Y:W)=(2:1:-3)$ satisfies \eqref{eq:C0102existence} and gives
\[
 (8,9/2,1,-5/2,-6).
\]
Multiplication by the rational cube $8$ preserves every cube condition and
gives the integer progression
\[
 (64,36,8,-20,-48),\qquad d=-28.
\]
Let $\alpha^3=6$.  The polynomial $T^3-6$ is Eisenstein at 2, so
$[\Q(\alpha):\Q]=3$, and
\[
 64=4^3,\qquad 36=(\alpha^2)^3,\qquad
 8=2^3,\qquad -48=(-2\alpha)^3.
\]
Thus positions $0,1,2,4$ are cubes.  Their classes modulo
$\langle[6]\rangle$ are $0201$; the permitted color automorphism
$c\mapsto2c$ gives the representative $0102$.

The omitted term is not a cube.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:kernel}, a nonzero
rational cube in $\Q(\alpha)$ has class $1$, $6$, or $36$ modulo rational
cubes.  Every such class has 5-adic valuation zero modulo 3, whereas
$v_5(-20)=1$.  All five displayed terms are nonzero and pairwise distinct,
so this is a legal nondegenerate four-hit progression.

For completeness, on $Y\ne0$ define
\[
 u=-2W/Y^2,\qquad v=2(X/Y)^3-1.
\]
Equation \eqref{eq:C0102existence} gives the exact identity
\[
 v^2=u^3+9.
\]
The source point maps to $(u,v)=(6,15)$.  This integral Weierstrass equation
has discriminant $-16\cdot27\cdot9^2=-34992$, and
$15^2=225\nmid34992$.  Nagell--Lutz therefore proves that $(6,15)$ is
non-torsion.  This proves positive rank of the quotient only; it does not
imply infinitely many rational lifts to the genus-four source.
\end{proof}

\section{Relation to earlier work and claim boundary}

Darmon--Merel and Hajdu--Tengely provide the two rational-power obstructions
used in the five-hit proof.  The closest methodological precedent we found is
Gonzalez-Jimenez--Xarles's treatment of five squares over quadratic fields
\cite{GJX}.  Their reduction already assigns Kummer types to the positions,
identifies common scaling and reversal, and studies the resulting curves over
$\Q$ by rational-point and local-solubility methods.  Thus we do not claim
originality for the general type/color, scaling, reversal, curve, or local-
obstruction strategy.  Their problem requires all five terms to be squares
over a quadratic field, whereas ours concerns exponent three, ternary
Kummer colors, partial hits, and a variable pure cubic field.

Other work on powers in number fields usually asks for all terms to be powers
of elements of the field: Gonzalez-Jimenez treats three cubes over quadratic
fields \cite{GJ}, Xarles proves qualitative degree-dependent bounds for
powers in progressions \cite{Xarles}, and Bremner--Siksek exclude five
squares over cubic fields \cite{BS}.  Hajdu--Tengely and
Bruin--Gyory--Hajdu--Tengely study literal powers in primitive integer
progressions \cite{HTold,BGHT}.  Those problems do not impose our combination
of rational AP entries, one common rational scale, partial hits, and one
variable pure cubic field.

Accordingly, the precise strengthened claim here is only Theorems
\ref{thm:main}, \ref{thm:0001}, \ref{thm:second3plus1}, and
\ref{thm:0102existence}: the exact five-term maximum, exhaustive exclusion
of all five-hit color classes, positive-rank infinite families in six stated
four-hit orbits, and strict existence in one further orbit.  We make no
priority claim and no classification claim for the other 24 four-hit models.
In particular, the unreviewed rank-zero quotient notes from Round 11 are not
part of any manuscript theorem.  The supplement reconstructs the 29-model
Round-09 input as four plane cubics, nine genus-$4$ bidegree curves, and
sixteen genus-$4$ cyclic covers; its coordinate-permutation clusters are not
asserted to be complete $\Q$-isomorphism classes.  Our search of accessible
publisher pages, arXiv records, zbMATH metadata, author publication pages,
the normalized progression, and the displayed genus-four equation located no
equivalent construction.  This is not a priority assertion.  MathSciNet was
blocked by institutional authentication, and the complete forward-citation
chain and full-text equation search remain to be checked.
